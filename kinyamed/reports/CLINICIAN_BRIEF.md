# Helping build a triage evaluation set — a brief for clinicians

**KinyaMed** is a research project testing whether software can help put patients in the right order in a
health-centre queue. It reads a patient's own description of their symptoms in Kinyarwanda, English, French or
Swahili. It is **not in use with any patient.** Today it cannot be trusted: we have no reliable way to test it.
That is what we need your help with.

## What we need from you

**Real clinical judgement on invented patient descriptions.** No real patients and no patient records are
involved. Nobody is diagnosed or treated. There are three roles, and you can take one or more, but never
annotate an item you wrote.

| Role | What you do | How much (Kinyarwanda first) |
|---|---|---|
| **Author** | Write short descriptions of symptoms, as a patient might say them, in your language, following a quota sheet (e.g. "a relative describing something you would triage as the highest urgency"). | ~1,300 descriptions across the authors |
| **Annotator** (2 people) | Read each description on a laptop and choose CRITICAL, URGENT, ROUTINE, or "cannot classify", plus how sure you are. You work alone and never see the other annotator's answer. | ~1,300 descriptions each |
| **Adjudicator** (1 person) | Decide the cases where the two annotators disagreed, and record why. | the disagreements only |

We also need **one lead clinician** to confirm, before we start:
1. which official triage guidance defines those three categories in Rwandan facilities;
2. the category definitions annotators will follow, taken from that guidance;
3. a handful of worked examples.

**We will not write these definitions ourselves.**

## How long it will take — estimates, to be measured

These are **assumptions, not measurements.** The first session is a pilot that measures the real time per item,
and we will re-plan from that.

| Step | Assumption | Estimate for Kinyarwanda (1,300 items) |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot (60 practice items, then a discussion) | ~2 hours | 2 annotators + lead clinician, one session |
| Writing items | 2–3 minutes per description | ~45–65 hours, shared across authors |
| Labelling | 30–45 seconds per description | **~11–16 hours per annotator**, in sessions of up to one hour |
| Adjudication | ~1 minute per disagreement; 15–25% disagree | ~3–5 hours |

The full four-language set is about five times larger (6,400 descriptions). We would only ask for it after the
Kinyarwanda set works.

## What happens to your work

- Your labels are stored under a code (for example "A1"), never your name, on a single laptop. Nothing is
  uploaded.
- Agreement between annotators is measured honestly. We report it even if it is low, and nobody is asked to
  change an answer to agree.
- The set is used only to test the software. A model that fails is reported as failing, and no system goes near a
  patient until it passes and has been approved separately.

## What we need from you before starting

1. **The triage guidance you use.** For example the WHO ETAT document in the edition your facility uses, the
   Rwanda MoH/RBC triage protocol, and any adult triage tool. We need the actual documents, not a summary.
2. **Whether your institution requires ethics or management approval** for this kind of work.
3. **A two-hour slot for the pilot.**
