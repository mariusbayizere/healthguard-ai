\section{The system}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Inference latency}

Measured on the reference machine --- an Intel i5-6200U with two threads
allocated to torch --- over \LatencyRows\ rows drawn from the frozen evaluation
split, at batch size one, which is the deployment shape:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrl}
\toprule
& latency & against a sub-200\,ms target \\
\midrule
Cold (first request, $n=1$) & \LatencyColdMs\,ms & exceeds \\
Warm median & \LatencyWarmMedianMs\,ms & meets \\
Warm p95 & \LatencyWarmPninetyfiveMs\,ms & meets \\
Warm p99 & \LatencyWarmPninetynineMs\,ms & meets \\
Warm maximum & \LatencyWarmMaxMs\,ms & exceeds \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The target is met warm through the 99th percentile on a nine-year-old laptop
CPU. It is not met in two conditions that are ordinary rather than exotic. The
first request a process serves costs \LatencyColdMs\,ms, roughly twenty times
the target, so any deployment that scales to zero or restarts a worker charges
that to a live patient; we load and warm the model at start-up for this reason.
And the warm tail reaches \LatencyWarmMaxMs\,ms, so a per-request service level
written at 200\,ms would be violated occasionally even in steady state. The p99
is the figure such a target should be written against.

\subsection{Service}

The triage endpoint accepts a free-text symptom description, detects the
language, classifies urgency, places the patient in a queue ordered by clinical
priority, and returns the label. The classifier sits behind an interface with
two implementations: a multilingual keyword baseline, and the fine-tuned model.
The model is selected by configuration and is not installed by default, because
the ML dependencies are deliberately absent from the API image; when it is
configured but unavailable the service logs a warning and continues on the
baseline rather than failing a request.

\paragraph{The patient-facing response is speaker-authored or absent.} The
endpoint returns the urgency label and a separate response slot. The sentence a
patient reads --- go to hospital now, see a
doctor today, it is safe to wait --- is the one piece of text in this system
that tells a person what to do about their health, and it is authored by a
speaker or it does not exist. The API states this explicitly rather than
supplying a placeholder: a response carries a \texttt{pending} flag and a reason
naming the brief that would fill it. The outbound SMS behaves the same way and
sends nothing while unauthored, on the reasoning that the patient is in the
queue either way and a machine-drafted urgency instruction in their hand is an
active harm rather than a missing convenience.

Speakers have since authored these for Kinyarwanda and Swahili --- three for the
endpoint and three for SMS in each --- so the service now answers a patient in
two of its four languages and returns an explicit pending state in the other
two. The slot was empty when the endpoint shipped, and shipping it empty rather
than filled with a plausible placeholder is what made the authored text
straightforward to drop in: nothing downstream had come to depend on a sentence
nobody wrote.

\paragraph{Deployment: \PENDING{not deployed}.} This system has not run in a
health centre and has not been used with any patient. A runbook exists; its
first precondition is that the model meet its own acceptance gate, which it does
not. Equally \PENDING{no community health worker consultation occurred}: the
corpus describes how one speaker believes patients present, and no observation
of real presentations informed it.

\subsection{Code-switching: none of the six combinations generates}

The corpus design names six ordered language pairs. \textbf{None of them
produces a row today}, and we state the position precisely because a corpus
labelled bilingual that is not is worse than one that admits the gap.

A Kinyarwanda speaker ruled fifteen medical terms for insertion, giving for each
whether it is genuinely switched or simply borrowed, its noun class, and the
agreement it triggers. That yielded five borrowed terms with established
Kinyarwanda forms, which belong in \emph{monolingual} rows rather than mixed
ones, and four cleanly switched terms.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.62\textwidth}}
\toprule
pair & why it does not generate \\
\midrule
kw $\rightarrow$ en & four switched terms are ruled, all nouns. Enough to emit
rows, not enough to be the mixed language the label claims. Pending a speaker's
answer on ten further terms. \\
kw $\rightarrow$ fr & no French-origin term is cleanly switched; all are
borrowed or dual-disposition. \\
en $\rightarrow$ kw, fr $\rightarrow$ kw & require the reverse insertion
direction and English or French frame fragments, neither of which exists. \\
sw $\rightarrow$ en, en $\rightarrow$ sw & require a Swahili-matrix worksheet.
None exists; the noun classes ruled so far are Kinyarwanda's. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Five of the fifteen terms carry a dual disposition --- the speaker's answer was
that they are both switched and borrowed, which is a real linguistic answer
rather than an incomplete one, but the two route a term to different places.
Two carry two candidate noun classes, and for one of those the two competing
forms sit in \emph{different} classes, so the choice is between two grammars
rather than two spellings.

\paragraph{Why the generator refuses rather than approximating.} The earlier
corpus produced mixed rows by swapping a whole phrase at one syntactic seam,
which accounted for 48\% of its rows and added no distinct clinical content. The
model that replaces it inserts a single content morpheme into an intact matrix
utterance and integrates it morphologically. That requires a noun class, and a
noun class is a fact about a language that cannot be inferred from the corpus.
The generator therefore declines to emit anything it cannot integrate, and
declines a pair whose inventory is too thin to be what the label claims.

Building the generator surfaced two errors it then prevented: an early version
filtered only on the embedded language and would have inserted
Kinyarwanda-classed terms into a Swahili frame, and a later one would have
shipped a four-noun inventory as a bilingual corpus.
