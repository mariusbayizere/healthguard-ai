\begin{abstract}
Triage decides who is seen first, and the instruments in general use assume a
shared clinical language between patient and assessor. In a Rwandan health
centre the patient speaks Kinyarwanda. We present a dataset and a classifier
for three-level urgency triage from patient-voice Kinyarwanda, and two
evaluation failures we consider more useful than the classifier.

The corpus is 330{,}000 rows generated from 165 distinct phrases across 96
clinical concepts, anchored to WHO guidance where guidance exists. It is
authored rather than translated: an earlier machine-generated corpus was judged
unusable by a Kinyarwanda speaker not for accuracy but for register and
grammatical form. Provenance is recorded per phrase and is mixed --- 82 of the
165 phrases (49.7\%) are authored directly by the speaker --- and we report that
split rather than describing the corpus as speaker-authored.

Our first evaluation split never worked: its manifest recorded that every one of
its 114{,}321 evaluation rows was built on a phrase also present in training,
in a machine-readable field, in the commit that created it. The two fields being
read were both honestly zero. Separately, a leakage guarantee that no
string-similarity rule could produce was needed, because a concept's
first-person and third-person phrasings share a prefix of zero characters by
construction; without an explicit declaration, 60 of 61 concepts had their two
phrasings on opposite sides of the holdout.

Our acceptance gate was a single condition, recall on the critical class. A
model passed it at \DegenerateRunCriticalRecall\ while achieving
\DegenerateRunUrgentRecall\ recall on the urgent class, and its critical-class
precision of \DegenerateRunPrecision\ sat below the \DegenerateCeiling\ that
merging the two classes earns for free on those rows. The gate certified the
worse of two models. We replace it with three conditions, two derived and one
declared unsourced.

The model we report reaches \ResultMacroFOne\ macro F1 and does not meet that
gate. We are not aware of a prior patient-voice triage dataset for Kinyarwanda,
though we have not searched systematically. The strongest constraint on every
number here is that \ResultEvalRows\ evaluation rows are frame permutations of
\ResultEvalPhrases\ distinct sentences, of which \ResultCriticalSentences\ carry
the critical label, so per-class recall moves in steps of roughly one quarter.
\end{abstract}
