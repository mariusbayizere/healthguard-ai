# Helping build a Kinyarwanda medical triage tool

## What this is

We are building a computer tool that reads how a patient describes their illness
and works out how urgently they need to be seen — critical, urgent, or routine. The
aim is to help health centres see the sickest patients first, when a nurse is
facing a full waiting room.

The important part: it must work in **Kinyarwanda**, not only English. Almost every
tool of this kind is built for English speakers, which means it is useless for most
people in Rwanda. That is the gap we are trying to close.

## The problem we have

The tool learns from examples of how people describe symptoms. Right now those
examples were **written by a computer, not by Rwandans**. They may be
grammatically odd, they may use words no patient would use, or they may sound like
a textbook rather than a person in a waiting room.

We cannot tell. Nobody who speaks Kinyarwanda has looked at them.

If the language is wrong, the tool will be wrong — and being wrong here means
possibly telling someone their chest pain can wait. So we would rather stop and ask
than carry on guessing.

## What we are asking you to do

Two things, both on a simple spreadsheet or on paper if you prefer.

**1. Check what we already have (about 46 phrases).**
For each one, tell us: would a patient actually say this? If not, what would they
say instead? You do not need to explain your reasoning unless you want to — your
correction is what matters.

**2. Write the phrases we are missing (about 80).**
We give you a short description in English — for example, *"a mother says her child
has stopped moving in her womb"* — and you write how a patient would say that in
Kinyarwanda. Two different ways of saying it is even better than one, because real
patients do not all use the same words.

We are **not** asking you to translate. Translation gives us textbook language. We
want the words people actually use, including informal ones, and including the
places where people mix in English or French because that is how they really speak.

## How long it takes

- Checking the 46 existing phrases: **about 1 hour**
- Writing the 80 new ones: **about 3 to 4 hours**

So roughly **half a day in total**. Most people find it easier in two or three
shorter sessions than in one sitting. There is no deadline and no requirement to
finish everything — even the first section is genuinely useful to us.

## What happens to your work

- Your corrections go straight into the tool. Nothing is used unchanged if you have
  flagged it as wrong.
- You will be **named as a contributor** in the project and in any paper that comes
  from it, unless you would rather not be.
- The dataset will be published openly for other researchers, so the phrases you
  write will help other people working on Kinyarwanda health language too.
- If at any point you want your contribution removed, say so and we will remove it.

## What we want you to be honest about

If a whole section looks wrong, tell us. If the urgency we have assigned to
something looks unsafe to you, tell us that too — a separate clinical review is
happening, but you may spot something they miss. If you think this approach cannot
work, that is the most useful thing you could tell us, and we would rather hear it
now than after the tool is built.

## What this is not

- Not a product being sold. It is research, published openly, free to use.
- Not in use with any real patient, and it will not be until clinicians say it is
  safe.
- Not a replacement for a nurse's judgement. It is meant to help sort a queue, not
  to diagnose anyone.
