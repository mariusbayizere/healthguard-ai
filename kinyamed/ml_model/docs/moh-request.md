# Request to the Rwanda MoH Community Health Desk

## What to ask for

Priority order.

1. **Community Health Worker training modules in Kinyarwanda** — the materials CHWs
   are trained on, particularly the modules covering danger signs and when to refer.
   These are the closest thing to a validated register of Kinyarwanda clinical
   language aimed at non-clinicians.
2. **Trainer's Guide on Integrated Management of Child Illness and Community Case
   Management (MoH, 2011)**, Kinyarwanda edition if one exists. Referenced in the
   literature; no download located.
3. **Patient education / health promotion materials in Kinyarwanda** — posters,
   leaflets, radio scripts. Radio scripts are especially valuable: they are written
   to be spoken, which is the register the model needs.
4. **The Kinyarwanda IMCI danger-sign wording** used in Rwandan practice. If MoH has
   an approved Kinyarwanda rendering of the general danger signs, that is
   authoritative in a way nothing drafted here can be.

Ask specifically about **permitted use**: whether the materials may be used to build
a publicly released, non-commercial research dataset with attribution.

## Where to send it

- **Community Health Desk**, Maternal Child and Community Health Division,
  Ministry of Health, PO Box 84, Kigali.
- **Rwanda Biomedical Centre (RBC)** runs implementation for community health
  programmes and may hold the training materials directly.

Uncertainty worth stating: the CHW programme has been under policy reform, so the
exact current office name should be confirmed before sending. Addressing both MoH
and RBC is the safer approach. This request also lands better from a Rwandan
colleague or an institutional address than from an unaffiliated individual.

## Draft wording

> Subject: Request for Kinyarwanda community health materials for a non-commercial
> research dataset
>
> Dear Sir/Madam,
>
> I am developing an open, non-commercial research dataset for multilingual medical
> triage in Kinyarwanda, English, French and Swahili, intended to support
> AI-assisted pre-screening at health centre level. The work is at an early stage
> and no clinical deployment is proposed.
>
> The dataset currently uses phrasings drafted by the project rather than language
> validated by Rwandan health practice, which limits its usefulness. I am writing to
> ask whether the Ministry can share, or point me to:
>
> 1. Community Health Worker training modules in Kinyarwanda, particularly those
>    covering danger signs and referral criteria;
> 2. the 2011 Trainer's Guide on Integrated Management of Child Illness and
>    Community Case Management, in Kinyarwanda if available;
> 3. patient education or health promotion materials in Kinyarwanda, including radio
>    scripts;
> 4. any approved Kinyarwanda wording for the IMCI general danger signs used in
>    Rwandan practice.
>
> I would also be grateful for guidance on permitted use: specifically whether these
> materials may be quoted or adapted in a dataset released publicly for
> non-commercial research, and how the Ministry should be attributed.
>
> I would welcome the opportunity to share the resulting dataset and any findings
> with the Ministry, and to have the clinical content reviewed by Rwandan clinicians
> before any publication.
>
> With thanks and respect,
> [name, affiliation, contact]

## Why this outranks drafting

Roughly 45,000 CHWs are trained on Kinyarwanda materials. That is a body of
patient-facing clinical language written and validated by Rwandan health
professionals. One usable module would be worth more than every Kinyarwanda phrase
drafted in this project, and would change the drafting task from authoring to
adapting.
