# Questions for a Rwandan English speaker

**47 rows across 22 concepts.** Each is English that is fine in general usage and
that I cannot verify for Rwandan usage. This document is the honest output of the
register review: the verdicts in the brief are a model's, and these are the ones
the model could not settle.

Grouped by the doubt rather than by domain, because the answer to each group
settles every row in it at once.

## The wording is British and may not be what is used here

| rows | wording | the doubt |
|---|---|---|
| OB01 f/t, IF02 f/t, EX33 t | **a fit** / *has had a fit* | British. East African English more often says *convulsions*, or *fits* in the plural. EX33 mixes both registers in one sentence — *has had a fit and is still convulsing*. |
| OB07 f/t | **my waters have broken** | British and plural. *My water has broken* or *the water broke* may be the local form. |
| PA07 t | **putting on weight** | British; *gaining weight* is the more neutral form. |
| EX06 f/t | **short of breath** | Idiomatic. *I cannot breathe well* is plainer and closer to the Kinyarwanda `guhumeka birangora`. |
| EX03 f/t | **my heart is racing** | Idiomatic. *My heart is beating fast* is plainer and closer to `umutima uratera cyane`. |

## The wording is mine, not anyone's

| rows | wording | the doubt |
|---|---|---|
| OB07 f/t, OB08 f/t | **my nine months are not yet complete** | **I invented this English idiom**, mirroring `igihe cyo kubyara ntikiragera` — the time to give birth has not arrived. It reads plausibly to me and I have no way to check it. If it is wrong it is wrong on four rows. |
| PR05 f/t | **I am newly pregnant** | May not be idiomatic. The concept is a first booking visit; *I have just found out I am pregnant* may be closer to how it is said. |
| HT05 f/t | **bent out of shape** | An idiom that may not carry. Both rows are held, so this flag lives only here. |

## The clinical word or the lay word?

| rows | wording | the doubt |
|---|---|---|
| EX26 f/t, IF04 f/t | **chills** | The Kinyarwanda says `imbeho` — feeling cold. *Chills* is the clinical word. Would a patient say chills, or cold? |
| EX22 f/t | **an infected wound** | *Infected* is a diagnosis, not an observation. A patient sees red, swollen and pus and infers the rest. |
| CR05 f/t | **a whistling sound** | The standard lay description of wheeze in British and American English. Whether a Rwandan patient reaches for it is exactly what the Kinyarwanda hold on `ijwi ridasanzwe` could not settle either — the same question, in a second language. |
| GI03 f/t | **black like tar** | A simile clinicians use to teach the sign. Would a patient reach for it, or just say black? |
| IF06 f/t | **pass urine** | Common in East African clinical English, but may be the health worker's register rather than the patient's. |
| PR09 f/t | **deworming medicine** | Programme vocabulary. A patient may say *worm medicine*, or name the tablets. |
| NE05 t | **cannot stand the light** | Idiomatic for photophobia. Also the row where the Kinyarwanda carries two of the concept's three signs. |

## Faithful to the Kinyarwanda, but possibly not to local English

These are the uncomfortable ones: the English is a good rendering of what the
speaker wrote, and local idiom may still differ.

| rows | wording | the doubt |
|---|---|---|
| CC06 f/t, CC07 f/t | **I have no HIV / TB medicine left** | Faithful to `Nta miti ya SIDA mfite`. But East African English strongly favours *my medicine is finished* or *the medicine got finished*, which the Kinyarwanda does not say. **Faithfulness and idiom pull apart here**, and I do not know which should win. |
| EX09 f/t, CC03 f/t | **blood pressure has gone very high** | In much East African English *pressure* alone carries it — *my pressure is high*. |
| EX35 f/t | **my mouth has gone crooked** | Renders `umunwa waragoramye` literally. Crooked, twisted, or pulled to one side — a usage question. |
| GI05 f/t | **blood in my stool** | *Stool* as a patient word. The speaker's Kinyarwanda avoids the noun entirely, because no stool word is attested for them, so there is nothing to check the register against. |
| PA08 f/t | **fluid coming out** (of the ear) | Kinyarwanda is vocabulary-blocked here too, so again nothing to check against. The ordinary phrase may be that the ear is *discharging*, or that *water* is coming out. |

## How to use this

Each group is one question, not one per row. A speaker who answers *"we say
convulsions, not a fit"* settles five rows. Nineteen of the 22 concepts have an
authored Kinyarwanda phrase beside them in the brief, which is the best available
cross-check while these are open.

Three of the groups — CR05, GI05, PA08 — are places where the **Kinyarwanda was
blocked on the same question**. Those are not coincidences: they are the concepts
whose vocabulary nobody has been able to settle in either language, and they
should go to the same person at the same time.
